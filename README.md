# D2RCabbyCodes

Cabby Codes for Diablo II: Resurrected: an in-game cheat panel for offline play.
Press F7, or open the game's Esc menu, and the panel is there.

- **God mode** - nothing takes life from you and nothing kills you: every way the game lowers a player's life is
  refused (hits, damage returned to you, crushing blow, poison, burning, Blood Mana), and a hit does nothing else to
  you either (no freeze, chill, stun or drain). Life is not refilled: it recovers as usual, and a life above its
  maximum (Battle Orders ended) still comes down to it.
- **Infinite mana** - your skills cost nothing, and the orb stays full.
- **Infinite stamina** - run without end.
- **Experience multiplier** - every experience gain is multiplied, 1x to 50x, kills and quest rewards alike.
- **Damage multiplier** - the damage you deal is multiplied, 1x to 100x: your attacks and spells, your summons' and
  your mercenary's, poison and burning included.
- **Movement speed** - up to +300 %, stacking with the game's own bonuses.
- **Invincible mercenary / invincible minions** - your mercenary, and everything you summon, take nothing from any
  hit.
- **Passive mercenary / passive minions** - your mercenary, and everything you summon, fight nothing: they follow
  you as always but never pick a fight, and do not hit back when hit. Their auras still work.
- **Permanent revives** - the monsters your Necromancer revives stay until they die: their three minutes never run
  out. The limit of revives (one a skill level) is still the game's.
- **Cannot be frozen / cannot be poisoned** - hits never freeze or chill you, and never poison you; a poison
  already on you ends at once.
- **Cannot be cursed** - curse immunity: a monster's curse never lands on you, and a curse already on you ends at
  once. Shrines work as always.
- **Infinite town portal, identify and potions** - scrolls stay after use, tomes keep their charges, and a potion
  you drink stays in its belt slot or inventory cell.
- **Infinite keys** - a key in your inventory opens every locked chest and door and is never spent.
- **No durability loss** - your weapon and armor never wear: not from your hits, the hits you take or Impale.
- **Infinite gold** - nothing you pay for takes your gold: buying, gambling, repairs, identifying, the mercenary.
- **Infinite imbues, sockets and personalizing** - Charsi imbues, Larzuk adds sockets and Anya personalizes whenever
  you ask and as often as you like, their quests done or not. A switch each.
- **Infinite cube ingredients** - a Horadric Cube recipe uses nothing up: what you put in comes back to your
  inventory beside what it made (at your feet when there is no room), and Clear Sockets gives back what was in the
  sockets too.
- **Respec** - reset every skill and attribute point, as a Token of Absolution does.
- **Skill and attribute presets** - save what a character spent its points on under a name, and load it back later
  (the character is reset and the points spent again).
- **Exit before death** - a hit that would kill you is stopped at 1 life and the game saves and exits to the
  character screen, so the death is never recorded. Optionally also when life falls below a percentage.
- **All areas terrorized** - every act is terrorized in every game, as if its Worldstone Shard had been used, and
  so is the Moo Moo Farm: every area a terror zone can take, for the whole game.
- **Map reveal** - the whole map of the area you are in is revealed on the automap, and so is every area you
  enter after it.
- **Landmarks on the map** - what the automap shows only once you have come near it - waypoints, shrines, wells,
  quest objects and chests, Act 3's sewer stairs and their lever - is on it as soon as you enter an area, wherever
  the area's layout places them.
- **Named enemies on the map** - unique and super unique monsters, bosses, champions and Heralds get a red cross on
  the automap with their name, however far away: super uniques and bosses from the moment you enter their area, the
  rest from the moment the game makes them (as you first come near their room), until they die.
- **Area names at exits** - the name of the area each exit leads to, on the automap: at a cave's entrance, a
  dungeon's stairs or a trap door, and at each opening where one area meets the next (the zone line between two
  outdoor areas, a town's gate), for the area you are in.
- **Area levels** - each area's monster level for the current difficulty after its name, in the automap's area
  text and in the waypoint list.
- **Item level** - an item's level after its name in its tooltip, as in "Grand Charm (91)": weapons, armor, rings,
  amulets, charms and jewels.
- **Monster health bars** - a short life bar floats above every enemy on screen: a blue frame for champions, gold
  for uniques and bosses. Town folk, your minions and your mercenary get none.
- **Boss health bar** - while an act boss is near (Andariel, Duriel, Mephisto, Diablo, Baal, their Uber versions and
  Diablo Clone), its name and life are shown at the top centre of the screen.
- **Loot filter drops** - monsters and chests drop only what your loot filter shows (the game's own filter, with the
  character's active profile), and every pick drops: a drop the filter would hide is made up for by one it shows,
  picked among everything the same treasure class can drop that the filter shows.
- **Chronicle drops** - the same for the unique and set items your Chronicle does not have yet: monsters and chests
  drop only those, each picked with the odds it has in the game, and a set item once a game. With the loot filter
  drops on too, a drop may be either: what the filter shows, or an item the Chronicle misses.
- **Extra drops** - up to 20 more items for every kill or chest, from the same treasure class; or, with its option
  on, only for the kills that tend to drop better: champions, uniques, bosses and Heralds.
- **Perfect rolls** - every new item's stats at the top of their ranges (drops, vendor stock, gambling, crafting,
  runewords, the cube), armor at its highest base defense, class items with three "+skill" lines at +3 each, and
  superior items with the best bonuses the game has for them, never above what the game itself can make.
- **All superior / all ethereal** - every new item that can be superior, or ethereal, is.
- **All socketed** - every new normal or superior weapon, body armor, helm and shield whose base can have sockets has
  them, as many as the game rolls: from 1 up to what the base allows at the item's level.
- **Max affixes** - every new magic, rare and crafted item has as many affixes as its quality can have: a magic item
  a prefix and a suffix, a rare item six (a rare jewel four), a crafted item four random ones. Which affixes is still
  the game's pick.
- **Best affixes** - every affix a new magic, rare or crafted item gets is the best version of that affix the item
  could have at its level: the same stats, the biggest numbers ("of the Whale" rather than "of the Jackal" on an
  amulet, "of Luck" on boots, "of Quickness" on a weapon). So is the automatic affix some bases come with (a paladin
  shield's all resistances, up to 45). Which affixes, and how many, is still the game's roll.
- **Kept between games** - when you load a character, the automap is open or closed, and Show Items and Show Items
  (Unfiltered) are on or off, the way they were when you last left a game.
- **Home town** - one town is your base: your town portals lead there, whatever act you read them in (the portal in
  that town takes you back to where you read it), and after a death you wake there.

Everything in the panel is a runtime change: no game file is modified, and every setting is changed from the panel
and remembered between sessions. The mod has no D2RMM settings.

Offline single-player only. Nothing here touches Battle.net characters, and the loader this runs on cannot
be used online anyway.

## Requirements

- Diablo II: Resurrected 3.3 (build 93847, the Battle.net client; the Steam build has the same code and should work, but is untested).
- [D2RLoader](https://d2rloader.net) 1.3.1 or newer. CabbyCodes is code that runs inside the game, and only the
  loader can put it there: D2RMM installs game data and cannot load a plugin, whichever folder it is given.
  Setting the loader up is a one-time step below; you keep installing your other mods with D2RMM as before.
- The Microsoft Visual C++ x64 runtime, 2019 (16.10) or newer - D2RLoader needs it. If D2RLoader shows an error
  about VCRUNTIME or MSVCP before the game opens, install
  [vc_redist.x64.exe](https://aka.ms/vs/17/release/vc_redist.x64.exe) from Microsoft.

## Install

One-time setup: put D2RLoader and the plugin into the game folder, then start the game through D2RLoader instead
of D2RMM's Run button. D2RMM stays your mod manager; only the launch changes.

1. **D2RLoader**: download the latest from [d2rloader.net](https://d2rloader.net) and unzip everything in it into
   the game folder - the one that holds `D2R.exe`, usually
   `C:\Program Files (x86)\Diablo II Resurrected`. `D2RLoader.exe` now sits next to `D2R.exe`.
2. **CabbyCodes**: open `D2RCabbyCodes_vX.Y.Z.zip` and drag the `d2rloader` folder inside it (it is in the
   `D2RCabbyCodes` folder) into the same game folder, so it merges with the loader's. You end up with
   `<game>\d2rloader\plugins\d2rl-cabbycodes.dll`.
   The same zip is the D2RMM mod folder: dropped into D2RMM's `mods` folder it is listed there, but the entry
   changes no game data, so enabling it and Install Mods do nothing by themselves.
3. **Launch**: make a desktop shortcut to `D2RLoader.exe` (right-click it, Send to, Desktop; on Windows 11 that
   is under Show more options). If you play with D2RMM mods, open the shortcut's Properties and add the Run
   button's arguments to the end of Target - hover D2RMM's Run button to see them, usually ` -mod D2RMM -txt`:

       "C:\Program Files (x86)\Diablo II Resurrected\D2RLoader.exe" -mod D2RMM -txt

   Install Mods in D2RMM as before, then start the game from this shortcut. D2RMM's own Run button starts the
   plain game, which skips the loader: your mods still load, but the panel will not be there.

Then load a character and press Esc (or F7): the Cabby Codes panel is there. If it is not:

- No `<game>\d2rloader\logs\d2rloader.log`, or one older than your launch: the game was not started through
  D2RLoader - use the shortcut.
- The log has no `CabbyCodes ... by SirCabby` line: the DLL is not in `<game>\d2rloader\plugins\`.
- D2RLoader shows an error before the game opens: see the Visual C++ runtime under Requirements.

**Updating**: replace `<game>\d2rloader\plugins\d2rl-cabbycodes.dll` with the new one. Your settings live in
`<game>\d2rloader\config\cabbycodes.toml` and stay. After a game patch, D2RLoader itself may need an update
before the game starts.

**Removing**: delete `d2rl-cabbycodes.dll`. To stop using the loader too, start the game from D2RMM or
Battle.net again; the loader's files sit idle until then.

**D2RMM for D2RLoader**: the [fork](https://www.nexusmods.com/diablo2resurrected/mods/1184) of D2RMM with the
loader built in can stand in for the shortcut - its Run Game starts `D2RLoader.exe`, it can download the loader
for you, and its Plugins tab imports `D2RCabbyCodes_vX.Y.Z.zip` directly. The game-folder install above works
the same under it.

### Linux (Proton)

The same files, inside the game's prefix. Start `D2RLoader.exe` with the same runner as the game and the same
arguments (`scripts/d2r-loader.sh` does this with umu-run; adapt its paths), with Proton's `run` verb rather than
`waitforexitandrun` - that one waits forever while Battle.net is open in the same prefix.

## Using it

- **F7** shows and hides the panel. With *Show on pause* on (the default) it also appears whenever the game's
  Esc menu is open, and F7 hides it for that visit.
- The panel has six collapsible sections: *Cheats* (god mode, infinite mana and stamina, speed, experience and
  damage multipliers, invincible and passive mercenary and minions, permanent revives, cannot be frozen, poisoned or
  cursed, infinite town portal,
  identify, potions and keys, no durability loss, infinite gold, infinite imbues, sockets and personalizing,
  infinite cube ingredients, all areas terrorized, exit before death),
  *Character* (respec,
  presets), *Loot* (loot filter drops, Chronicle drops, extra drops, perfect rolls, all superior, all ethereal, all
  socketed, max affixes, best affixes),
  *Display* (map reveal, landmarks, named enemies and area names at exits on the map, area levels, item level, health
  bars), *Quality of
  life* (the automap and Show Items kept
  between games, your home town) and *Diagnostics* (the log switched on or off, and how severe a line must be to be written).
  Hover an option or a (?) mark for details.
- Settings are written to `cabbycodes.toml` the moment you change them.
- The D2RLoader console (Ctrl+` when enabled in the loader's settings) has a `cabbycodes` command:
  `status`, `panel`, `god|mana|stamina|exit|bars|bossbar|map|ilvl on|off`, `landmarks|enemies|exits on|off`
  (landmarks, named enemies and area names at exits on the map), `exp <multiplier>`, `dmg <multiplier>`,
  `speed <percent>`, `merc|minions|unfreezable|unpoisonable|uncursable|tp|id|potions|keys|gold|durability on|off`,
  `passivemerc|passiveminions on|off` (your mercenary or your summons fight nothing),
  `revives on|off` (your revives stay until they die),
  `imbue|addsockets|personalize on|off` (Charsi's, Larzuk's and Anya's service without its quest),
  `cube on|off` (cube recipes use nothing up),
  `home <0..5>` (your home town: 0 the game's own, 1 to 5 that act's town), `respec`,
  `preset [save|load <name>]`, `tz on|off`, `loot|chronicle|perfect|superior|eth|sockets|affixes|best on|off`,
  `drops <count>`,
  `elites on|off`, `keepmap|keepitems|keepunfiltered on|off`, `log on|off`, `loglevel info|warning|error`, `save`,
  `sites`, `dropcheck [walks]` (a check of the
  loot filter drops' odds against the game itself, see below), `perf [seconds]` (times the plugin's own work while
  you play, 10 seconds unless you say how long, and writes what each part of it took to the log).

## Good to know

- **Exit before death** arms once your life is above the trigger (1 life, or the percentage) and fires on the
  fall. A character loaded at 1 life will not be thrown out on entry. While the exit is in flight every lethal
  hit is held at 1 life. `force_close` is the fallback if the loader cannot deliver the Save and Exit; it keeps
  the character alive at its last save.
- **All areas terrorized.** A Worldstone Shard terrorizes its act for the rest of the game: the game applies the
  act's own zone (every area of the act a terror zone can take) as a "manual" terror, which the half-hour rotation
  never takes off. With the switch on, the plugin does the same for all five acts in every game, and for the Moo Moo
  Farm (which no shard covers), and applies a zone again if its areas ever lose the terror. The rotation carries on
  underneath; its zone takes over again when you switch this off, which ends the manual terror in the game you are
  in (a shard you used yourself included). Areas you enter from then on generate terrorized; an area already visited
  in the current game keeps its monsters as they were. It needs a game with terror zones: Baal killed on that
  difficulty, and the game's offline terror zones enabled in its options.
- **Character badges.** D2RLoader tags characters played with mods ("soft modded") and plugins ("plugins") on
  the character screen, from a sidecar file next to the save. The save itself is untouched.
- **Hardcore.** Exit before death works the same in hardcore; a death that never happens is never recorded.
  It is still a cheat plugin, not a guarantee.
- **Other plugins.** All hooks into the game go through D2RLoader, which tracks and chains them. If another
  plugin owns a routine CabbyCodes needs, that cheat is greyed out with the reason in the panel.
- **Health bars** read what the game's own client knows: a monster's life is a fraction there, so the bars and the
  boss bar show percentages, not points. They need HD graphics (in legacy graphics only the boss bar shows), hide
  under an open inventory or character panel, and hide while the Esc menu is open. A boss joins the boss bar within
  about two screens of you and leaves it a little further out.
- **Loot filter drops** use the game's own loot filter, with the profile chosen for the character and its rules
  exactly as the game applies them: a show rule wins over a hide rule, and quest items always drop. With
  the filter on, every pick of a monster's or chest's treasure class drops (there is no NoDrop). Nothing is rolled
  again and again until the filter is pleased. What the treasure class can drop is worked out instead: every item
  it leads to with how often the game's walk of it comes to that item, every quality the game's quality roll can
  give the item with its chance (your magic find and the treasure class's bonuses counted as the game counts
  them), and what the item generator makes of that (a unique that came already this game is rare instead, some
  items can be ethereal or come with sockets). What the filter hides is struck from that list, and the rest keep
  the odds they have against each other in the game. The game's own walk still decides how many items drop and
  rolls each of them once; an item the filter hides in every form is not made, one it shows in some forms (a base
  it shows only when ethereal or socketed, a unique whose rare stand-in it hides) is made in one of those, by their
  chances, and each pick that came to nothing is made up for by one pick from the list, as are the extra drops.
  So what drops is what rolling again and again would have dropped, without the rolls, and a pick stays empty
  only when the monster or chest can drop nothing (more) that the filter shows. A unique item still drops only once
  a game, as in the plain game: a filter that shows a list of uniques has fewer of them left the longer a game goes
  on, and a new game starts the list again. Gold goes by its amount, which the filter is asked about once the pile
  is there. While the loot filter editor is open, drops are not filtered. Barrels, urns, bodies and weapon racks
  count as chests. Every item made this way is checked with the game's own filter test; should the odds ever not
  hold (a game update), the log says so and drops are rolled for as before. `cabbycodes dropcheck` in the loader's
  console has the game walk the treasure class of the next monster you kill 5000 times with nothing made (a short
  hitch) and sets what it asked for against the odds worked out: the log says whether they are the game's.
- **Chronicle drops** go by the game's own Chronicle, the record of the unique and set items (and runewords) you have
  found that its Chronicle panel shows: the entries it lists that your character's Chronicle does not have yet. They
  work the way the loot filter drops do, with those items in place of what the filter shows: every pick drops, a
  pick of something else is made up for by one of them picked with the odds it has in the game, and the game is
  told which unique or set item to make, so it makes that one with everything the item generator gives it. A pick
  stays empty when the monster or chest can drop none of them (more). A unique drops once a game in the game itself,
  and a set item the Chronicle misses drops once a game too, so extra drops do not bring the same piece again and
  again; if it stays on the ground, it can drop again in the next game. An item goes into the Chronicle when it is
  identified. With the loot filter drops on too, a drop may be either (a unique or set item of the list, or
  whatever the filter shows), so a filter that shows gold and a few bases keeps those coming beside the missing
  items. Gold and everything else only the filter can let through, and quest items always drop. Runewords are made,
  not dropped: the Chronicle's runewords are left to you. The Chronicle is read from the game's memory, the way the
  game's own routines read it, and nothing of it is written.
- **Extra drops** can be kept to the kills that tend to drop better (*Only from champions, uniques, bosses and
  Heralds*): champions, unique and super unique monsters, a terror zone's Heralds, and the monsters the game's data
  marks as bosses - the act bosses and their Uber versions, Diablo Clone, Blood Raven, Radament, the Summoner,
  Izual, Griswold, Nihlathak and the colossal Ancients. Ordinary monsters get none then, nor do a unique's or a
  Herald's minions (they drop as ordinary monsters do), nor chests, barrels, urns, bodies and racks. The loot filter
  switch still covers every drop.
- **Perfect rolls** cover every property value (affixes, uniques, sets and their bonuses, runewords, crafted and
  cube results, superior mods) and armor base defense. A class item (one only one class can use: an Amazon's bow or
  javelin, an orb, a shrunken head and so on) also rolls how many "+skill to one skill" lines it gets and how high
  each is: with perfect rolls it gets three, each +3, by the game's own rolls (an inferior one stays at +1, as in the
  game). A superior item has one of a few kinds of bonus, which the game picks at random among those that go on
  the item (an armor's may be durability alone); with perfect rolls it is the best of them: enhanced defense with
  durability on armor, enhanced damage with attack rating on a weapon (the game has no kind with all three of a
  weapon's bonuses), 15 / 15 and 15 / 3. Which skill a random-skill property gives, which skills a class item gets,
  which class a Hellfire Torch serves, which skill tree Wraithstep's "+1 to ... Skills" names, and which stat a
  random property picks stay random,
  as do socket counts, stack sizes, gold, the durability a drop comes with, and which affixes an item has. The cube's
  upgrade recipes remake an item from its seed, so upgraded items come out perfect too.
- **All superior / all ethereal** apply to every freshly made item: drops, vendor stock, cube results. An item the
  cube remakes (an upgrade) keeps what it was. The game's own limits stay: potions, gems and runes are never
  superior; set and inferior items, and anything the game makes never ethereal (vendor stock, gambling, quest
  items), are never ethereal. Ethereal items cannot be repaired.
- **All socketed** works where the game itself rolls sockets: new normal and superior items (drops, vendor stock,
  cube results) whose base can have them at the item's level - weapons, body armor, helms and shields, class items
  included. Only the game's one-in-three chance is skipped: the number is still its own roll, from 1 up to the base's
  maximum for the item level (item levels 1-25, 26-40 and 41 and up allow more), and the game caps it at 3 sockets
  in Normal and 4 in Nightmare. Throwing weapons, gloves, boots, belts, jewelry, and magic, rare, set, unique and
  crafted items are unchanged, and so are quest items, a new character's starting gear and an item the cube remakes.
- **Max affixes** works after the game's own roll: the game decides how many affixes a new item gets (a magic item a
  prefix, a suffix or both; a rare item 3 to 6, a rare jewel 3 or 4; a crafted item 1 to 4 random ones, more at
  higher item levels), and the item then gets more, one at a time, from the game's own affix picker until it has the
  most the game ever gives its quality: 2, 6 (three prefixes and three suffixes), 4 for a rare jewel, and 4 random
  ones for a crafted item (its recipe's own properties come on top, as usual). Each affix still follows the game's
  rules: one the item's type and level allow, one per affix group, rare-only affixes on rares. An item with fewer to
  choose from (a low item level, a base with few affixes) gets fewer, as it would in the game. Drops, vendor stock,
  gambling and cube results; not uniques, set items or runewords (fixed properties), quest items, or an item the
  cube remakes (it keeps its affixes). Items made in a classic (non-expansion) game get it on magic items only.
- **Best affixes** works on each affix the game picks: the game still decides which affix comes (and max affixes how
  many), and the plugin then keeps the best version of it. An affix's versions are the rows of its affix group that
  give the same stats (the four attack speed tiers, 10 % to 40 %, count as one affix; so do run/walk, hit recovery
  and cast rate): of those the game could have picked for that item, by its own rules (the item's type, its affix
  level, rare-only affixes on rares, what is spawnable), the one with the biggest numbers is kept. So every item stays
  one the game could drop there, with the level requirement that version has: an item from a low area gets the best
  its level allows, and in Hell almost every affix is its top version. Values are still rolled in their range
  (perfect rolls puts them at the top). The automatic affix some bases come with is kept at its best version the
  same way: a paladin shield's all resistances (+35-45 from item level 75 on a Sacred Targe; a rare one +25-35, the
  game's best for rares) or its attack rating with enhanced damage, whichever the game picked; an orb's life or mana,
  a Necromancer head's poison, an Amazon bow's or spear's skills, a Warlock grimoire's magic or fire damage. Drops,
  vendor stock, gambling and cube results; not uniques, set items, runewords, quest items, an item the cube remakes,
  or items made in a classic (non-expansion) game.
- **The seed saved with an item.** The game draws two random numbers when it makes an item. The first rolls the
  durability the item comes with, an armor's base defense and the picture of a ring, amulet, charm or jewel, and it
  is the only one a save file keeps. The second rolls everything the item generator decides (which affixes, every
  value, ethereal, sockets), and no save keeps it. The switches above act in the generator, on that second stream,
  so they change nothing the saved seed rolled. The one thing the saved seed does roll that a switch cares about is
  an armor's base defense: under perfect rolls a new armor piece is made from a seed that rolls the top of its base's
  range, and the game's own item creation rolls the durability, the defense and the picture from it. Nothing is
  written to the item afterwards, so the seed in the save agrees with the item. Finding such a seed takes about a
  hundred multiplications (a tenth of a microsecond) inside the item's creation: nothing is searched in the
  background, waited for or kept in a file.
- **Damage multiplier** scales a hit before the game adds it up, so resistances and immunities still apply: an
  immune monster stays immune. It covers your attacks, spells and missiles, your summons and your mercenary, and
  the poison and burning they leave.
- **Invincible mercenary / minions** take everything a hit carries away before it lands: damage, poison, burning,
  chill, freeze, stun, and the mana or stamina it would drain. The hit still counts as a hit, so "when struck"
  effects still fire. A poison already on them when the switch goes on runs its course.
- **Passive mercenary / minions** leave each pet's own AI running, with nothing to fight: every way it looks for a
  target finds none, so it does what it does without an enemy - it follows you, buffs, eats corpses (a Carrion Vine
  still heals you). Its aura still works (Holy Freeze still chills, a Fire Golem's Holy Fire still burns, an Iron
  Golem's thorns still return damage), it does not hit back when hit, and an attack it had begun when the switch went
  on still lands. Traps and hydras do nothing while it is on. Hostile monsters are untouched: they still attack your
  pets, which the invincible switches keep alive.
- **Cannot be frozen / poisoned** take the freeze and chill, or the poison, off every hit on you. Cold damage
  itself still hurts. Cannot be poisoned also ends a poison already on you, the way an antidote potion does (the
  green life orb turns red again). Cannot be frozen, like the game's own "cannot be frozen", does not end a freeze
  already on you.
- **Cannot be cursed** keeps off what the game's data calls a curse that can be cured: Amplify Damage, Weaken, Dim
  Vision, Taunt, Iron Maiden, Terror, Attract, Life Tap, Confuse, Decrepify, Lower Resist, Defense Curse and Blood
  Mana. They come from the monsters that cast them (the Oblivion Knights, the succubi and their witches, Baal, the
  Summoner) and from unique monsters with the Cursed modifier. The game counts a shrine's bonus among the curses
  too, since a curse ends a shrine's bonus and a shrine ends a curse; those cannot be cured and are left alone, so
  every shrine works as it always does. Your own curses on monsters are untouched, and so are your mercenary and
  your summons: a curse still lands on them.
- **Infinite gold** is "nothing is taken", not "you have it all": the game checks that you hold the price, in your
  inventory or your stash, before it sells, repairs or hires, and that check stays. What does not happen is the
  payment. It covers everything a vendor takes gold for: buying (a whole stack too), gambling, repairs, Deckard
  Cain's identifying, hiring a mercenary and bringing one back to life. Selling pays you as usual, and gold you drop,
  lose when you die or put in the stash moves as usual.
- **Infinite imbues, sockets and personalizing.** Three of the game's people do something to an item as a quest's
  reward, once: Charsi imbues (Tools of the Trade), Larzuk adds sockets (Siege on Harrogath), Anya personalizes
  (Betrayal of Harrogath). With a service's switch on, its NPC has the entry in the menu at every talk, the quest
  done or not, and does it as often as you ask. The quest itself is left alone: a reward you have earned and not
  used is still there when you switch the option off, and a quest you have not done can still be done. What an item
  gets is the game's own. Imbue makes a plain item (low quality, normal or superior) without sockets a rare one of
  your character's level plus four (the loot options act on it as on any new item). Add Sockets gives a normal or
  superior item the most sockets its base can have at its level, a magic item one or two, a rare, set, unique or
  crafted item one; an item that has sockets gets no more. Personalize puts your character's name on the item. You
  still have to reach the NPC: Larzuk is in Harrogath, and so is Anya once you have freed her (Prison of Ice).
  Switch an option on before you talk to the NPC: the menu is made as the talk begins.
- **Infinite cube ingredients.** A recipe still needs its ingredients in the cube, and makes what it makes; with the
  option on, what it would have used up comes back to your inventory as the recipe finishes, beside what it made in
  the cube. With no room in the inventory an item is dropped at your feet (as a quest reward is when your inventory
  is full). Clear Sockets (Hel, a Scroll of Town Portal and a socketed item) gives back the gems, runes and jewels
  that were in the sockets too, where the game destroys them. An item a recipe changes in place - an upgrade to
  exceptional or elite, added sockets, a repair, the item Clear Sockets empties - is what the recipe makes: it stays
  in the cube, changed, and is not copied. The portal recipes (the Secret Cow Level, Pandemonium, the red portal)
  keep their keys, organs and Wirt's Leg; whether a portal opens is still the game's own rule.
  Quest recipes (the Horadric Staff, Khalim's Will) give their parts back too.
- **Home town.** With a town chosen, a Scroll or Tome of Town Portal read in any act opens a portal to that town,
  and the blue portal that stands there takes you back to where you read it, in whichever act that was. The portal
  closes when you come back through it, as always. After a death you wake in that town too, where the game wakes
  you in it, with your portal to where you died still standing there if you had one open; your body lies where
  you died. A town can be home only when the character has its waypoint: the game's own record of the acts a
  character has reached. For a town it has not, and in the act whose town is home, portals and wakes are as they
  always are. One thing to know: a portal read in Tal Rasha's Chamber once Duriel is dead leads to Lut Gholein
  whatever is chosen, because the game puts that portal at a spot of its own. Portals a quest opens, the portals to
  the Moo Moo Farm and the Uber levels, the travel the act's people offer, and the town a game starts in are
  untouched.
- **Infinite town portal, identify and potions** work from every place an item is used: the inventory, the belt
  (and its 1-4 keys), the town portal hotkey, and a potion given to the mercenary. Merging two tomes, buying
  scrolls and putting a scroll on a tome still count as usual. Quest items (the Potion of Life, the Book of Skill,
  Anya's scroll) are used up as ever.
- **Infinite keys** needs one key: it must be in the inventory (not the stash or the cube), the place the game
  takes a key from, and with none there a locked chest still says it needs one. Assassins open chests without keys
  anyway; locked doors ask them too.
- **No durability loss** stops the wear before it is rolled. The game wears a player's weapon, now and then, when a
  melee hit lands, and a piece of armor when the player is hit in melee; Impale wears its weapon (a stack of javelins
  loses one instead). None of that happens to your gear, ethereal items included. Nothing is repaired: an item
  already worn stays worn, and a broken one stays broken until an NPC repairs it. Arrows, bolts and thrown weapons
  still run out as you use them. The game never wears a mercenary's gear.
- **Map reveal** reveals an area as you enter it (a large one fills in over a few frames), once per visit to an
  act. It is the game's own automap, as if you had explored the whole area. Only the map is revealed: monsters,
  chests, shrines and waypoints show on it once you have been near them, as usual.
- **Area names at exits** are found as you enter an area: a warp (a cave's entrance, stairs, a trap door) by the
  game's own link between its room and the area at its other end, a border by where its rooms meet the next area's
  and where a player can walk across. Where two areas meet, each names the other on its own side, and only the
  names of the area you are in are shown. The way down from Act 3's Sewers Level 1 is the stairs the lever opens,
  which no link of the area names: Landmarks on the map shows the stairs and the lever instead.
- **Kept between games.** One state for every character: the last one you left a game with. It is put back while
  the game loads, the way its key would change it (as if you pressed Tab or the Show Items key), so it is there when
  the loading screen goes away. The two Show Items options need the game's own display option for that key (Item
  Name Display, Unfiltered Item Name Display, in the game's options) set to Toggle; in Hold or Timed mode nothing
  is kept, and the panel says so. D2RLoader itself keeps the automap and Show Items (not the unfiltered one) within one run of the game, and
  on the first game of a run it closes the map, overriding the game's own "open on load" option; the automap option
  here keeps the map after the game is closed too.
- **Respec** is the Token of Absolution's own reset, run from the panel: every skill point and attribute point the
  character spent comes back to spend again. Quest rewards already received stay received.
- **Presets** keep a character's base attributes and the points in each skill (not what items add), per class.
  Loading one resets the character first, then spends the points the way the preset says, prerequisites first,
  with every check the game makes when you click: a skill whose level requirement is still above you, or points
  the character does not have yet, are left to spend.
- **The Esc menu pauses the game**, and a respec or a preset load changes the character in the game itself, so
  from the Esc menu they happen the moment you close it (the panel says so, and can cancel). Saving a preset
  happens at once. With the panel opened by F7 while you play, everything happens at once.
- **Game updates.** Routines are found by byte signature, never by fixed address. On a new game build each
  cheat either resolves or is greyed out; nothing is patched blind.
- **Item level** is the game's own item level display, switched from the panel: the level goes after the name the
  way the game adds it for items whose data asks for it (the `ShowLevel` column that Show Item Level sets), on items
  you already have too. Quest items and throwing potions never show it, and neither do runes, gems, potions and
  the like. Show Item Level is not needed alongside; with it enabled, the items it marks show their level whether
  the switch is on or off.

## How it works

The plugin hooks a handful of the game's own routines through the loader:

| Routine | Used for |
|---|---|
| player stat regeneration (every server frame) | the tick: status, refills, the speed bonus, arming exit before death |
| damage application and the kill decision | god mode (a hit on you does nothing but its damage, which your life cannot lose), holding a lethal hit at 1 life, the damage multiplier, invincible mercenary and minions, cannot be frozen or poisoned |
| "may this stat go down?" (asked by every stat writer before it lowers a value) | god mode: the answer is no for a player's life, whatever lowers it |
| the player death penalties | last line of defence: leave before the death is counted |
| skill mana cost | infinite mana: the local player's skills cost 0 on the server and in the client's prediction |
| add experience | the multiplier scales the gain before the game adds it, so level-ups follow |
| the client's level-name lookup, while the automap or the waypoint panel asks | area levels appended to the names (the levels come from the game's own Levels table) |
| the treasure-class drop routine (monsters and chests) | loot filter and Chronicle drops (every pick; a pick the filter hides, or the Chronicle has, made up for by one it shows or misses, which the routine is asked for with a treasure class of one entry) and extra drops |
| item creation | loot filter and Chronicle drops: a drop is made only in a form the filter shows or as an item the Chronicle misses (the request says which unique or set item, whether ethereal, whether with sockets), and one of neither in every form is not made (the drop routine gets no item, as it does when there is no room for one) |
| the item generator's quality step | all superior, all ethereal and all socketed, through the generator's own request (its rules still decide) |
| the property roll | perfect rolls: the game still rolls, and the value becomes the top of its range |
| item creation | perfect rolls: a new armor is made from a seed (the one a save keeps) that rolls its top base defense, through the routine's own "use the request's seeds" argument |
| a class item's skill lines | perfect rolls: the step's own bonus argument raises its rolls, so it gives three lines at +3 |
| "does this kind of superior bonus go on the item?" (asked by the generator's superior step of each kind its roll comes to) | perfect rolls: the answer is no for every kind but the best that goes on the item, so the step comes to that one and makes it itself |
| the item generator's magic, rare and crafted affix steps | max affixes: after the game's own pass, the game's affix picker adds affixes until the item has the most its quality can have |
| the item generator's affix picker | best affixes: the game picks, then the best version of that affix the item could have is kept (the rare step's two picks are pointed at this picker, which the other steps already call) |
| the item generator's automatic affix picker (a base's own affix, such as a paladin shield's all resistances) | best affixes: the game picks, then the best version of that affix the item could have is kept; the generator applies it as usual |
| "is a used item removed?" (asked by the client's use request and the server's check) | infinite scrolls and potions: the answer is no, on both sides |
| a tome's quantity update | infinite tomes: the charge a use takes is not taken |
| the key use (a locked chest or door asks it for a key) | infinite keys: the lock opens and the key stays, when you carry one |
| a hit's wear on a weapon or armor piece, and Impale's wear on its weapon | no durability loss: the wear is not rolled for your gear |
| a curse skill's step for one unit in its range, and the Cursed monster modifier's | cannot be cursed: the step is not made for you, so nothing is put on |
| the payment every vendor takes its price with | infinite gold: it answers "paid" and takes nothing |
| a town portal's cast, the maker of the portal at its destination, and a portal's use | home town: the portal at the destination is made in the home town (its act made first when no player has been there in this game), and a portal that leads into another act takes you there with the game's own move to a level, the one waypoints take |
| the server's handler of a dead player's request to wake | home town: the game wakes you in the town of the act you died in, as always, and you are taken on to the home town with the same move |
| the client's test whether it goes through a town portal ahead of the server (it only reads) | home town: for a portal whose other end stands in another act the answer is no, so the client asks the server and waits, as it does for every portal used outside a town |
| the client's NPC menu, as it opens | infinite imbues, sockets and personalizing: the game's own routine that puts the service in the NPC's menu is called, whatever the quest says |
| the cube's product routine, the free of an item, and the free of what is in an item's sockets | infinite cube ingredients: inside a transmute, the two frees the product routine makes of what it used up are not made; when it is through, each item goes to your inventory the way the game hands you a quest reward (else at your feet, as the game drops one) |
| a monster's AI tick, the test whether a unit is another's enemy (which every way an AI looks for a target asks), and the getter of a target kept on a monster | passive mercenary and minions: while a passive pet's AI runs, no unit is its enemy and no target is kept for it, so it finds nothing to fight; any other question gets the game's answer |
| the timer of the "killself" monster modifier (the end of a revive's time; a Decoy's and a "Reanimate As" monster's too) | permanent revives: for one of your revives the timer is not run, so nothing ends it; the game's own pet lookup says which of your pets it is, and every other monster's timer is the game's |

All areas terrorized hooks nothing: from the tick it applies each act's zone with the routine a Worldstone Shard's
use calls (and tells the clients the way the shard does), and takes it off with the routine the half-hour rotation
uses for its own zone.

The three NPC services hook nothing on the server. The game keeps a table of its NPCs that says which item service
each one does and which quest asks for it; its handler of the request tests the quest only for an NPC whose record
names one, and does the service at once for one that names none. While a service is switched on, the quest is taken
out of its NPC's record (one byte, from the tick), and put back when the option goes off or the plugin unloads. No
quest record is read or written, so nothing of it reaches a save.

Which states are curses comes from the game's States table: a state flagged both as a curse and as one that can be
cured. A curse that is on you all the same is ended from the tick with the antidote potion's own cure, as a poison
is. The home town reads each act's town and its waypoint from the game's ActInfo and Levels tables, and asks the
game's own waypoint test which of them the character has.

Respec and presets call the game's own routines rather than hooking anything: the Token of Absolution's reset, and
the server's handlers for the "spend a point" requests the client sends when you click a + button, so every check
the game makes applies. Which items are scrolls, tomes and potions comes from the game's Items table. Cannot be
poisoned ends a poison already on you with the antidote potion's own cure: the routines its use effect calls.

The loot filter test is the game's own (the same one its ground labels use), asked about each kind of item before
a drop is made and about every item that is. What a treasure class drops is read from the game's own tables (the
treasure classes, ItemRatio, the unique and set items), and what only the game can say (whether a treasure class's
condition holds for the monster at hand) is asked of its drop routine. What the Chronicle misses is read where the
game keeps it: its own lists of the uniques and set items that are entries, and the character's record of those
found.
The health bars hook nothing. Once a frame, on the game's UI thread (the loader's UI callback), they walk the
monsters the client knows, keep the ones the game itself calls hostile, read each one's life, and ask the game where
it draws above that monster's head (the routine its own overhead text uses). The game's MonStats table says which
monsters are bosses and which are town folk. The bars are drawn with the same overlay as the panel. They are the
one part that works at every frame, as the bars follow what moves, and only while there is something to follow: in
town, outside a game or with both switches off a frame costs them a few comparisons.

The map reveal hooks nothing either. On the same UI thread it reads which area you are in from the client's own copy
of the map, waits until the automap has moved to that area's layer, then builds each room of the area with the
game's own room builder and hands it to the automap's own "reveal this room" callback, the one the game uses to
reveal a room whole. It looks when the loader says you entered an area or a game (and once a second, should an area
not be announced), not at every frame; while an area is being revealed the work goes on from frame to frame. The
landmarks, the named enemies' spawn spots and the area names at exits are read in the same pass, from what the room
builder makes: a room's preset units, its links to the areas next to it, its collision map. The named enemies and
the exits' names are drawn by one hook, on the automap's own draw of a unit, with the game's own marker and name
routines.

Keeping the automap and Show Items between games hooks nothing. On the UI thread, while a game runs, it reads whether
the automap panel is open and whether each Show Items key is on, from the same places the game's own getters read:
when the game's own UI messages say a key or a button was pressed or a panel came or went, and once a second besides.
When a character is loaded it presses the key whose state differs, through the game's own table of key actions (the
function the game runs for Tab or the Show Items key, D2RLoader's wrapper of it where the loader has one, so the
loader's own memory of the map and Show Items agrees).

Movement speed goes into the player's base `velocitypercent` stat, so skills and items stack on top as usual.
Save and Exit sends the same UI message the pause menu's button sends. See `CLAUDE.md` for the record.

The item level uses the game's own display. For an item whose `ShowLevel` column is set, the client's item-name
routine adds the level after the name; a hook on that routine adds it the same way for the items the switch
covers. The server, though, tells the client an item's real level only for items with `ShowLevel`, and a
placeholder 1 for the rest, so one branch of its item writer becomes a jump (through the loader, for the session):
every item's real level is sent, the same seven bits as before. Saves are written by another branch and do not
change.

## Building

Cross-compiled on Linux with mingw-w64 (`x86_64-w64-mingw32-g++`, C++20). No MSVC needed.

```
cp config.mk.example config.mk    # set GAME_DIR (and D2RMM_MODS_DIR)
make                              # developer build -> build/dev/d2rl-cabbycodes.dll
make install                      # copy it into <game>/d2rloader/plugins/
make test                         # host-side unit tests (run under wine)
make dist                         # release build -> dist/D2RCabbyCodes/ (the D2RMM mod folder)
make package                      # the release zip, dist/D2RCabbyCodes_vX.Y.Z.zip
```

Vendored: Dear ImGui (MIT), MinHook (BSD-2, only for the DXGI Present hooks the overlay needs), the D2RLoader
PluginSDK headers (MIT). Research credits: RuffnecKk's D2RLoader Suite (MIT) for the 3.2/3.3 address corpus.

The item level follows olegbl's Show Item Level.
