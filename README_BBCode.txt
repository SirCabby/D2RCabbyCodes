[size=5][b]D2RCabbyCodes[/b][/size]

Cabby Codes for Diablo II: Resurrected: an in-game cheat panel for offline play. Press F7, or open the Esc menu, and the panel is there.

[list]
[*][b]God mode[/b] - nothing takes life from you and nothing kills you: every way the game lowers a player's life is refused (hits, damage returned to you, crushing blow, poison, burning, Blood Mana), and a hit does nothing else to you either. Life is not refilled: it recovers as usual, and a life above its maximum (Battle Orders ended) still comes down to it.
[*][b]Infinite mana[/b] - skills cost nothing, the orb stays full.
[*][b]Infinite stamina[/b] - run without end.
[*][b]Experience multiplier[/b] - every gain multiplied, 1x to 50x, kills and quests alike.
[*][b]Damage multiplier[/b] - the damage you, your summons and your mercenary deal, 1x to 100x, poison and burning included. Resistances and immunities still apply.
[*][b]Movement speed[/b] - up to +300 %, stacking with the game's own bonuses.
[*][b]Invincible mercenary / invincible minions[/b] - your mercenary, and everything you summon, take nothing from any hit.
[*][b]Passive mercenary / passive minions[/b] - your mercenary, and everything you summon, fight nothing: they follow you as always but never pick a fight, and do not hit back when hit. Their auras still work.
[*][b]Permanent revives[/b] - the monsters your Necromancer revives stay until they die: their three minutes never run out. The limit of revives (one a skill level) is still the game's.
[*][b]Cannot be frozen / cannot be poisoned[/b] - hits never freeze or chill you, and never poison you; a poison already on you ends at once.
[*][b]Cannot be cursed[/b] - curse immunity: a monster's curse never lands on you, and a curse already on you ends at once. Shrines work as always.
[*][b]Infinite town portal, identify and potions[/b] - scrolls stay after use, tomes keep their charges, a potion you drink stays in its belt slot or inventory cell. Quest items are used up as ever.
[*][b]Infinite keys[/b] - a key in your inventory opens every locked chest and door and is never spent.
[*][b]No durability loss[/b] - your weapon and armor never wear: not from your hits, the hits you take or Impale.
[*][b]Infinite gold[/b] - nothing you pay for takes your gold: buying, gambling, repairs, identifying, the mercenary.
[*][b]Infinite imbues, sockets and personalizing[/b] - Charsi imbues, Larzuk adds sockets and Anya personalizes whenever you ask and as often as you like, their quests done or not. A switch each.
[*][b]Infinite cube ingredients[/b] - a Horadric Cube recipe uses nothing up: what you put in comes back to your inventory beside what it made (at your feet when there is no room), and Clear Sockets gives back what was in the sockets too.
[*][b]Respec[/b] - reset every skill and attribute point, as a Token of Absolution does.
[*][b]Skill and attribute presets[/b] - save what a character spent its points on under a name and load it back later: the character is reset and the points spent again, with every check the game makes.
[*][b]Exit before death[/b] - a hit that would kill you is stopped at 1 life and the game saves and exits to the character screen. The death is never recorded. Optional life-percent threshold.
[*][b]All areas terrorized[/b] - every act is terrorized in every game, as if its Worldstone Shard had been used, and so is the Moo Moo Farm: every area a terror zone can take, for the whole game.
[*][b]Map reveal[/b] - the whole map of the area you are in is revealed on the automap, and so is every area you enter after it.
[*][b]Area levels[/b] - each area's monster level after its name in the automap's area text and the waypoint list.
[*][b]Item level[/b] - an item's level after its name in its tooltip, as in "Grand Charm (91)": weapons, armor, rings, amulets, charms and jewels.
[*][b]Monster health bars[/b] - a short life bar above every enemy on screen (blue frame for champions, gold for uniques and bosses). Town folk, your minions and your mercenary get none.
[*][b]Boss health bar[/b] - while an act boss is near (Andariel, Duriel, Mephisto, Diablo, Baal, their Uber versions, Diablo Clone), its name and life at the top centre of the screen.
[*][b]Loot filter drops[/b] - monsters and chests drop only what your loot filter shows (the game's own filter and the character's profile), and every pick drops: a drop the filter would hide is made up for by one it shows, picked among everything the same treasure class can drop that the filter shows.
[*][b]Extra drops[/b] - up to 20 more items for every kill or chest, from the same treasure class; or, with its option on, only for the kills that tend to drop better: champions, uniques, bosses and Heralds.
[*][b]Perfect rolls[/b] - every new item's stats at the top of their ranges (drops, vendor stock, gambling, crafting, runewords, the cube), armor at its highest base defense, class items with three "+skill" lines at +3 each, and superior items with the best bonuses the game has for them, never above what the game itself can make.
[*][b]All superior / all ethereal[/b] - every new item that can be superior, or ethereal, is.
[*][b]All socketed[/b] - every new normal or superior weapon, body armor, helm and shield whose base can have sockets has them, as many as the game rolls: from 1 up to what the base allows at the item's level.
[*][b]Max affixes[/b] - every new magic, rare and crafted item has as many affixes as its quality can have: a magic item a prefix and a suffix, a rare item six (a rare jewel four), a crafted item four random ones. Which affixes is still the game's pick.
[*][b]Best affixes[/b] - every affix a new magic, rare or crafted item gets is the best version of that affix the item could have at its level: the same stats, the biggest numbers ("of the Whale" rather than "of the Jackal" on an amulet, "of Luck" on boots, "of Quickness" on a weapon). So is the automatic affix some bases come with (a paladin shield's all resistances, up to 45). Which affixes, and how many, is still the game's roll.
[*][b]Kept between games[/b] - when you load a character, the automap is open or closed, and Show Items and Show Items (Unfiltered) are on or off, the way they were when you last left a game.
[*][b]Home town[/b] - one town is your base: your town portals lead there, whatever act you read them in (the portal in that town takes you back to where you read it), and after a death you wake there.
[/list]

The panel is runtime only: no game file is modified. Settings change from the panel and are remembered; the mod has no D2RMM settings. Offline single player only.

[size=4][b]Requirements[/b][/size]
[list]
[*]Diablo II: Resurrected 3.3 (build 93847).
[*][url=https://d2rloader.net]D2RLoader[/url] 1.3.1 or newer. CabbyCodes is code that runs inside the game, and only the loader can put it there: D2RMM installs game data and cannot load a plugin. Setting the loader up is a one-time step; you keep installing your other mods with D2RMM as before.
[*]The Microsoft Visual C++ x64 runtime, 2019 (16.10) or newer ([url=https://aka.ms/vs/17/release/vc_redist.x64.exe]vc_redist.x64.exe[/url]) - D2RLoader needs it.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Install[/b][/size]
One-time setup: put D2RLoader and the plugin into the game folder, then start the game through D2RLoader instead of D2RMM's Run button. D2RMM stays your mod manager; only the launch changes.

[b]1. D2RLoader:[/b] download the latest from [url=https://d2rloader.net]d2rloader.net[/url] and unzip everything in it into the game folder - the one that holds D2R.exe, usually C:\Program Files (x86)\Diablo II Resurrected. D2RLoader.exe now sits next to D2R.exe.
[b]2. CabbyCodes:[/b] open D2RCabbyCodes_vX.Y.Z.zip and drag the d2rloader folder inside it (in the D2RCabbyCodes folder) into the same game folder, so it merges with the loader's. You end up with <game>\d2rloader\plugins\d2rl-cabbycodes.dll. The same zip is the D2RMM mod folder: dropped into D2RMM's mods folder it is listed there, but the entry changes no game data, so enabling it and Install Mods do nothing by themselves.
[b]3. Launch:[/b] make a desktop shortcut to D2RLoader.exe. If you play with D2RMM mods, open the shortcut's Properties and add the Run button's arguments to the end of Target - hover D2RMM's Run button to see them, usually -mod D2RMM -txt. Install Mods in D2RMM as before, then start the game from this shortcut. D2RMM's own Run button starts the plain game, which skips the loader: your mods still load, but the panel will not be there.

Then load a character and press Esc (or F7): the panel is there. If it is not: no <game>\d2rloader\logs\d2rloader.log (or one older than your launch) means the game was not started through D2RLoader - use the shortcut; no "CabbyCodes" line in that log means the DLL is not in d2rloader\plugins; an error before the game opens usually means the Visual C++ runtime above is missing.

To update, replace d2rl-cabbycodes.dll - your settings stay in d2rloader\config\cabbycodes.toml. To remove, delete it.

[url=https://www.nexusmods.com/diablo2resurrected/mods/1184]D2RMM for D2RLoader[/url], the fork of D2RMM with the loader built in, can stand in for the shortcut: its Run Game starts D2RLoader.exe, it can download the loader for you, and its Plugins tab imports D2RCabbyCodes_vX.Y.Z.zip directly.

[size=4][b]Using it[/b][/size]
F7 shows and hides the panel; with "Show on pause" on it also appears with the Esc menu. Collapsible sections: Cheats (god mode, infinite mana and stamina, speed, experience and damage multipliers, invincible and passive mercenary and minions, permanent revives, cannot be frozen, poisoned or cursed, infinite town portal, identify, potions and keys, no durability loss, infinite gold, infinite imbues, sockets and personalizing, infinite cube ingredients, all areas terrorized, exit before death), Character (respec, presets), Loot (loot filter drops, extra drops, perfect rolls, all superior, all ethereal, all socketed, max affixes, best affixes), Display (map reveal, area levels, item level, health bars), Quality of life (the automap and Show Items kept between games, your home town), Diagnostics (the log switched on or off, and how severe a line must be to be written). Settings are saved to d2rloader\config\cabbycodes.toml the moment you change them, presets to cabbycodes.presets.txt beside it. The loader console has a "cabbycodes" command (status, god|mana|exit|bars|bossbar|map|loot|perfect|superior|eth|sockets|affixes|best|merc|minions|passivemerc|passiveminions|revives|unfreezable|unpoisonable|uncursable|tp|id|potions|keys|gold|imbue|addsockets|personalize|cube|durability|tz|ilvl|keepmap|keepitems|keepunfiltered|log on|off, loglevel info|warning|error, exp <x>, dmg <x>, speed <pct>, home <0..5>, drops <n>, elites on|off, respec, preset save|load <name>, save, dropcheck).

[size=4][b]Good to know[/b][/size]
[list]
[*]Exit before death arms once life is above the trigger and fires on the fall, so a character loaded at 1 life is not thrown out. If the loader cannot deliver Save and Exit, the fallback closes the game; the character keeps its last save, alive.
[*]Loot filter drops: nothing is rolled again and again until the filter is pleased. What a treasure class can drop is worked out: every item, every quality with its chance (magic find counted as the game counts it), and what the generator makes of it (ethereal, sockets, a rare in place of a unique that came already). What the filter hides is struck from that, the rest keep the odds they have against each other in the game, and every pick the filter would hide is made up for by one pick among them. What the filter hides is not made at all. A pick stays empty only when the monster or chest can drop nothing (more) that the filter shows. A unique item still drops only once a game, as in the plain game, so a filter that shows a list of uniques has fewer of them left the longer a game goes on. Nothing is filtered while the loot filter editor is open. Barrels, urns, bodies and racks count as chests. "cabbycodes dropcheck" in the loader's console checks the odds against the game itself (the log has the outcome).
[*]Extra drops can be kept to the kills that tend to drop better ("Only from champions, uniques, bosses and Heralds"): champions, unique and super unique monsters, a terror zone's Heralds, and the monsters the game's data marks as bosses (the act bosses and their Uber versions, Diablo Clone, Blood Raven, Radament, the Summoner, Izual, Griswold, Nihlathak, the colossal Ancients). Ordinary monsters get none then, nor do a unique's or a Herald's minions, nor chests, barrels, urns, bodies and racks. The loot filter switch still covers every drop.
[*]Perfect rolls cover every property value (affixes, uniques, sets, runewords, crafted and cube results, superior mods) and armor base defense. The random picks stay random (which skill a random-skill property gives, which class for a Hellfire Torch, which skill tree for Wraithstep, which stat a random property picks), and socket counts, stack sizes, gold and the durability a drop comes with too. A class item gets three "+skill" lines, each +3 (an inferior one stays at +1, as in the game). A superior item gets the best of the kinds of bonus the game has for it: enhanced defense with durability on armor, enhanced damage with attack rating on a weapon.
[*]All superior / all ethereal apply to every freshly made item (drops, vendor stock, cube results) and keep the game's limits: potions, gems and runes are never superior; set and inferior items, vendor stock, gambling and quest items are never ethereal. Ethereal items cannot be repaired.
[*]All socketed works where the game itself rolls sockets: new normal and superior weapons, body armor, helms and shields. Only the game's one-in-three chance is skipped: the number is still its own roll, from 1 up to what the base allows at the item's level, and the game caps it at 3 in Normal and 4 in Nightmare. Throwing weapons, gloves, boots, belts, jewelry, magic and better items and a new character's gear are unchanged.
[*]Max affixes works after the game's own roll: the item gets more affixes, one at a time, from the game's own affix picker, until it has the most the game ever gives its quality (2 for magic, 6 for rare with three prefixes and three suffixes, 4 for a rare jewel, 4 random ones for crafted besides the recipe's). An item with fewer to choose from at its level gets fewer, as in the game. Not uniques, sets, runewords, quest items or an item the cube remakes. In a classic (non-expansion) game only magic items get it.
[*]Best affixes works on each affix the game picks: the game still decides which affix comes (and max affixes how many), then the best version of it is kept. An affix's versions are the rows of its affix group that give the same stats (the attack speed tiers, 10 % to 40 %, count as one affix; so do run/walk, hit recovery and cast rate); of those the game could have picked for that item by its own rules (the item's type, its affix level, rare-only affixes on rares), the one with the biggest numbers wins. Every item stays one the game could drop there: an item from a low area gets the best its level allows, and in Hell almost every affix is its top version. The automatic affix some bases come with is kept at its best version the same way: a paladin shield's all resistances (+35-45 from item level 75 on a Sacred Targe; a rare one +25-35, the game's best for rares) or its attack rating with enhanced damage, whichever the game picked; an orb's life or mana, a Necromancer head's poison, an Amazon bow's or spear's skills, a Warlock grimoire's magic or fire damage. Drops, vendors, gambling and the cube; not uniques, sets, runewords, quest items, an item the cube remakes, or items made in a classic (non-expansion) game.
[*]The seed saved with an item: the game draws two random numbers when it makes an item. The first rolls the durability the item comes with, an armor's base defense and the picture of a ring, amulet, charm or jewel, and it is the only one a save keeps. The second rolls what the item generator decides (affixes, values, ethereal, sockets), and no save keeps it. The switches above act in the generator, so they change nothing the saved seed rolled. Under perfect rolls a new armor piece is made from a seed that rolls its top base defense, by the game's own item creation: nothing is written to the item afterwards, and the seed in the save agrees with it. Finding such a seed is a tenth of a microsecond's arithmetic: nothing is searched in the background, waited for or kept in a file.
[*]Cannot be cursed keeps off what the game's data calls a curse that can be cured: Amplify Damage, Weaken, Dim Vision, Taunt, Iron Maiden, Terror, Attract, Life Tap, Confuse, Decrepify, Lower Resist, Defense Curse and Blood Mana, from the monsters that cast them and from unique monsters with the Cursed modifier. The game counts a shrine's bonus among the curses too (one ends the other); those are left alone, so every shrine works as it always does. Your mercenary and your summons can still be cursed.
[*]Infinite gold is "nothing is taken", not "you have it all": the game checks that you hold the price, in your inventory or stash, before it sells, repairs or hires, and that check stays. It covers buying (a whole stack too), gambling, repairs, Deckard Cain's identifying, hiring a mercenary and bringing one back to life.
[*]Infinite imbues, sockets and personalizing: Charsi (Tools of the Trade), Larzuk (Siege on Harrogath) and Anya (Betrayal of Harrogath) each do something to an item as a quest's reward, once. With a service's switch on, its NPC has the entry in the menu at every talk, the quest done or not, and does it as often as you ask. The quest itself is left alone: a reward you have earned and not used is still there when you switch the option off. What an item gets is the game's own: Imbue makes a plain item (low quality, normal or superior) without sockets a rare one of your character's level plus four; Add Sockets gives a normal or superior item the most sockets its base can have at its level, a magic item one or two, a rare, set, unique or crafted item one (an item that has sockets gets no more); Personalize puts your character's name on it. You still have to reach the NPC: Anya is in Harrogath once you have freed her (Prison of Ice). Switch an option on before you talk to the NPC: the menu is made as the talk begins.
[*]Infinite cube ingredients: a recipe still needs its ingredients in the cube and makes what it makes; with the option on, what it would have used up comes back to your inventory as the recipe finishes (at your feet when there is no room, as a quest reward is dropped when your inventory is full). Clear Sockets gives back the gems, runes and jewels that were in the sockets too, where the game destroys them. An item a recipe changes in place (an upgrade, added sockets, a repair, the item Clear Sockets empties) is what the recipe makes: it stays in the cube, changed, and is not copied. The portal recipes keep their keys, organs and Wirt's Leg; whether a portal opens is still the game's own rule. Quest recipes give their parts back too.
[*]Home town: with a town chosen, a Scroll or Tome of Town Portal read in any act opens a portal to that town, and the blue portal there takes you back to where you read it; it closes when you come back through it, as always. After a death you wake in that town too, your portal to where you died still standing there if you had one open; your body lies where you died. Only a town the character has the waypoint of can be home: for another, portals and wakes are as they always are. A portal read in Tal Rasha's Chamber once Duriel is dead leads to Lut Gholein whatever is chosen (the game puts that portal at a spot of its own). Quest portals, the travel the act's people offer and the town a game starts in are untouched.
[*]Infinite town portal, identify and potions work from the inventory, the belt and its 1-4 keys, the town portal hotkey, and a potion given to the mercenary. Merging tomes and buying scrolls count as usual.
[*]Infinite keys needs one key in the inventory (not the stash or the cube), where the game takes a key from; with none, a locked chest still says it needs one.
[*]No durability loss stops the wear before it is rolled (your weapon on melee hits, your armor when you are hit, Impale's wear), ethereal items included. Nothing is repaired: a broken item stays broken until an NPC repairs it. Arrows, bolts and thrown weapons still run out as you use them.
[*]All areas terrorized works the way a Worldstone Shard does, for all five acts and the Moo Moo Farm at once: the terror lasts the whole game (the half-hour rotation never ends it) and comes back in every game. Switching it off ends it in the game you are in, a shard you used yourself included. Areas you enter from then on generate terrorized; areas already visited keep their monsters. It needs a game with terror zones: Baal killed on that difficulty, and offline terror zones enabled in the game's options.
[*]Map reveal reveals each area as you enter it (a large one fills in over a few frames). It is the game's own automap, as if you had explored the whole area; monsters, chests, shrines and waypoints show on it once you have been near them, as usual.
[*]Kept between games: one state for every character, the last one you left a game with, put back while the game loads as if you pressed the key. The two Show Items options need the game's Item Name Display (and Unfiltered Item Name Display) option set to Toggle. D2RLoader itself keeps the automap and Show Items within one run of the game (and closes the map on the first game of a run); this keeps them after the game is closed too, and adds the unfiltered one.
[*]Presets are per class and keep base attributes and skill points (not item bonuses). Loading resets the character and spends the points again, prerequisites first; points the character cannot spend yet (a lower level, fewer quest rewards) are left to spend.
[*]The Esc menu pauses the game: a respec or a preset load chosen there happens the moment you close the menu (saving a preset happens at once). From the F7 panel while playing, everything happens at once.
[*]Health bars need HD graphics (in legacy graphics only the boss bar shows). Bars under an open inventory or character panel are hidden, and everything hides while the Esc menu is open.
[*]D2RLoader tags characters played with mods and plugins on the character screen (a sidecar file next to the save; the save itself is untouched).
[*]All hooks go through D2RLoader. If another plugin owns a routine, that cheat is greyed out with the reason. Routines are found by signature, never by fixed address, so a game update greys cheats out instead of breaking anything.
[*]Item level is the game's own item level display, switched from the panel: it shows on items you already have too, never on quest items, throwing potions, runes, gems or potions. Show Item Level is not needed alongside; with it enabled, the items it marks show their level whether the switch is on or off.
[/list]
